Yoga poses for children

Facilitator creates a story using animal yoga poses (cat, cow, cobra, monkey, etc.) and children's names from the group.

5–10 minutes
Age group
Toddlers (1–2 years), Preschoolers (3–4 years), School age (5+ years)
Season
Fall, Winter, Spring, Summer
Setting
Both indoors and outdoors
Number of children
One child Small group (2–5 children) Medium group (6–10 children)
View activity

How it works

Facilitator creates a story using animal yoga poses (cat, cow, cobra, monkey, etc.) and children's names from the group. Each child follows doing the poses on their mat that are demonstrated by the facilitator. Warm up and cool down with belly breathing, using the stuffies on children's tummies, so that they can see them rise and fall as they breathe.

Make it easier

Spend a few days just learning the poses, and photograph children doing the poses, to post around the room (for practice, and while following the story).

Make it more challenging

Have older children anticipate the name of the pose from the demonstration, or demonstrate the pose from the name (if familiar with yoga poses).

Activity Support

Adult involvement: Facilitator (1 adult)

Inclusion strategies: For physical disabilities, modify poses, and/or have another ECE on hand to assist. For behavioral issues, keep poses simple and the story brief, and have additional staff available.

Category: Portable play equipment

Equipment and materials: Mats, Other portable play equipment (please specify):; Stuffies

Animal Movement- Obstacle

It's a great activity for toddlers and preschoolers to move their bodies with minimum planning/set up.

5–10 minutes
Age group
Toddlers (1–2 years), Preschoolers (3–4 years), School age (5+ years)
Season
Spring, Summer
Setting
Both indoors and outdoors
Number of children
Large group (11+ children)
View activity

How it works

It's a great activity for toddlers and preschoolers to move their bodies with minimum planning/set up. It supports body awareness, coordination, balance, self-regulation and many other areas and skills. I would have animal movement visuals, music playing and a variety of loose parts set up for the kiddos as an obstacle course to increase it and make it tricky for them as a safe risk.

Make it easier

This activity is great for a wide range of ages and can easily be modified to support each child’s individual needs. Whether a child has one arm, one leg, uses a wheelchair, has a cast, or requires other accommodations, educators can adapt the activity in an inclusive and meaningful way so every child can participate successfully. One of the benefits of this activity is that it requires very little setup, time, or materials, making it flexible and easy to adjust as needed. For example, if a child uses a wheelchair, they could help hold up the animal picture cards for the group, participate with a partner during movements, or engage in modified movements that are appropriate and comfortable for them. Educators would thoughtfully select and adapt movements based on each child’s individual abilities and needs to ensure all children feel included, capable, and engaged.

Make it more challenging

As mentioned above I would increase the materials to make it more challenging for older children, I could also add instructions "Use one hand, one foot" to make it tricky and take a little longer as a challenge and to expand on the learning.
